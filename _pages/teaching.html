--- layout: archive title: "Teaching" permalink: /teaching/ author_profile: true --- {% include base_path %} My pedagogical approach is grounded in disability justice, alongside the work of femme scholars of color such as Leah Lakshmi Piepzna-Samarasinha, Karen L. Suyemoto, and Audre Lorde. Drawing on their insights, I teach relationally and in community with my students; during the first class of the semester in each of my courses, I name how Western models stigmatize emotion in the classroom and define teaching through hierarchical control, rather than collective meaning-making. To counter this, I approach the classroom as a space of mutual translation, where knowledge is built through collaborative processes that allow for vulnerability, and for intellectual risk-taking that allows students to stumble as they grow. I draw from transformative justice scholarship oand consent educators/organizers in this work, and scaffold structures in place throughout the semetser to address if/when hurt or harm occurs in the classroom. As an educator, I feel that it is one of my primary jobs to ensure my students have as much power and agency over their learning as I can give them.
